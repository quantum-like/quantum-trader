Replication report

Do market regimes improve machine-learning stock ranking?

Reproducing Pascual Miralles & Alfeus (SSRN 7200998, 2026) with the authors' public code and freely available substitute data in place of Bloomberg.

Verdict

Paper values in muted gray throughout; reproduced values in blue. Test window 2021–2025, 60 monthly rebalances, equal-weight Top10 of 206 large U.S. stocks ranked by a 7-seed XGBoost ensemble on predicted 20-day return rank percentile.

Out-of-sample growth of $1

The paper's benchmark rows are matched to the third decimal (SPY CAGR 14.72% vs 14.71%, drawdown −20.47% both), which confirms the rebalance calendar, holding-period arithmetic and total-return SPY reconstruction are identical. The Top10 line compounds less steeply than in the paper.

Table 1 — Portfolio performance, 2021–2025

Calendar-year returns

The paper reports the Top10 portfolio beating SPY in every test year. Reproduced, it beats SPY in 2021, 2022 and 2025 and trails narrowly in 2023–2024, the years dominated by the mega-cap AI rally.

Table 2 — Ranking diagnostics

Every diagnostic keeps its sign and ordering; the point estimates are roughly 55–65% of the paper's and the p-values move from the 1–5% range into the 7–15% range. The Bottom20 average monthly return matches exactly (1.16%).

Table 3 — Alternative learners

Same features, target, split and portfolio rule; only the learner changes. Extra Trees and Ridge land within a point of the paper, LightGBM and Random Forest within 3–6 points, and every tree ensemble beats SPY at the 5% level. Two things differ: XGBoost is the weakest tree model here rather than the strongest (the paper's own paired tests found the tree models statistically indistinguishable), and the neural network reaches 30% CAGR against the paper's 16%. The MLP is a single early-stopped fit, so that number is the least stable in the table.

Regime experiments (Tables 10–13)

The paper's central claim is negative: HMM regimes help describe when the model works but do not improve it as a feature or as a basis for regime-specific models. That replicates. Adding one-hot regime indicators lowers the CAGR slightly (24.3% vs 25.0%) and weakens the IC; training one model per regime leaves the portfolio unchanged but cuts the ranking IC to 0.010 (p = 0.66), consistent with the paper's sample-fragmentation explanation. Neither paired difference is significant in either study. Both specifications are reconstructed from the paper's Section 4.5 because the public repo omits that script.

Baseline conditional on the prevailing regime (Table 12)

HMM state numbers are arbitrary and the economic labels are assigned by the authors' heuristic from each state's return, volatility, drawdown, VIX and yield-spread averages. The paper's point is that the strategy's performance varies strongly by state; that replicates (CAGR ranges from −16% to +60% across states). Which state is best does not: the reproduced HMM's highest-stress state is a high-volatility recovery phase rather than a 2008/2020-style crisis, and the strategy does best there.

What the model uses (SHAP)

The paper reports firm size as the dominant predictor, followed by valuation ratios, MACD and moving-average ratios, profitability, leverage and long momentum, with gold and aggregate-bond returns contributing. The reproduced ordering is the same in structure.

Why the Top10 edge is smaller

The benchmark, the bottom-ranked portfolio, the panel sizes (653k / 102k / 257k rows vs 654k / 103k / 254k) and the linear Ridge model all match the paper closely. What differs is the top of the ranking, where a boosted-tree model leans on point-in-time fundamentals.

Bloomberg's daily market cap, P/E, P/B, ROE and D/E are replaced here by values derived from SEC XBRL filings (2009–2025) and a stockpup mirror (2004–2008). These are true point-in-time series and market caps land within 0–3% of known values, but ratio definitions, earnings normalizations and the treatment of negative-equity names differ from Bloomberg's. SHAP shows P/B and D/E in the top four and P/E lower than the paper reports, consistent with the P/E proxy being noisier.

The paper's own robustness tables show the headline is sensitive to specification (walk-forward retraining 23.0%, regime models 23.4%, no cross-asset features 31.3%), so a 25% CAGR from vendor-substituted inputs sits inside the range the authors themselves report.

Data provenance

This environment could reach only GitHub, so every input series was rebuilt from public repositories that mirror Yahoo Finance, LBMA, EIA, Treasury.gov and SEC EDGAR data. Each file's URL and SHA-256 is recorded in reproduction/raw_sources/manifest.json.
