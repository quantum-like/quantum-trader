# Reconciling six AI replications of SSRN 7200998

*"Do Market Regimes Improve Machine-Learning Stock Ranking?" (Pascual Miralles & Alfeus, 2026).* Six AI-driven replications were run on public data because the paper's Bloomberg inputs cannot be redistributed. This note lines them up against the paper and each other, explains why they differ, and states what can and cannot be concluded. Every number below was checked against its source document by an independent review pass; qualifications from that pass are folded in.

Sources (Slack DM with Alan Coppola, 2026-09-25): Grok message (16:25); ChatGPT screenshot (18:03) and `chatGpt.zip` (18:12); a "Sherry's model" label (18:09) with no numeric attachment; Kimi table (18:16); a 988 MB `replication_results.zip` (18:31, provenance not stated — it sits between the "perplexity" label and the Perplexity screenshot); Perplexity screenshot (18:33) and `REPLICATION_REPORT.md` (18:42, marked *interim*: its alternative-model and walk-forward runs were still going); Claude Code artifact (21:58); "claude science" `replication_report-2.md` (22:01). Two reconciliation messages were already sent to Alan at 23:21 and 23:24; this note supersedes them with verified figures and three new re-tests.

## 2. What the runs agree on, where tested

1. **The Top10 portfolio beats SPY in the point estimate, by less than the paper says.** Six of six runs: 19.3–25.0% CAGR against 14.7% for SPY, i.e. an excess of 4.6–10.3 CAGR points versus the paper's 19.5 (the paper's "17.24% excess" is an annualized mean-return figure). SPY alpha 1.5–8.9% versus 13.6%. Top−Random is positive in every run that reports it but never below p 0.067.
2. **The paper's 5%-level significance is not robust.** With each run's closest match to the paper's specification, no Top−SPY, Top−Random, Top−Bottom or IC test is below 0.05 in any run: Top−SPY p 0.061–0.318 (paper 0.005); IC p 0.092–0.490 in the four runs that report it (Grok says "n.s.", Kimi reports none). Nominal 5% results do appear once something is changed — one of Perplexity's five seed sets and one of Claude Code's four (§4); LightGBM and Random Forest in claude science, LightGBM/Random Forest/Extra Trees/neural network in Claude Code, Extra Trees only in ChatGPT; Kimi's variant; claude science's 2008–2018 training control (p 0.007); the FF5+MOM alphas of Perplexity and ChatGPT — and none of them survives the within-family multiple-testing corrections that claude science and ChatGPT applied.
3. **Regimes add nothing significant.** Four runs tested regime-aware forecasting (ChatGPT, Perplexity, claude science, Claude Code; Grok did not because the released repo lacks the script, Kimi reported none). Paired annualized differences for "HMM regime as a feature" against each run's own baseline: −0.8 (Claude Code), +0.2 (ChatGPT), +1.0 (Perplexity), +2.2 to +4.4 (claude science, depending on the label-switched vs state-aligned regime series and on whether pre-2008 rows are dropped); the paper's −2.9. None is significant at 5% (p 0.055–0.97). One model per regime: 12.2% (ChatGPT), 18.0% (Perplexity), 23.0–26.6% (claude science), 25.1% (Claude Code) against baselines of 21.5–25.0%, never significantly different, and with a weaker IC in every run that reports one (Claude Code's per-regime IC is 0.010 against 0.031).
4. **No optimizer significantly beats 1/N inside the Top10.** Five runs tested it (Grok, ChatGPT, Perplexity, claude science, Claude Code; Kimi none). Which optimizer is worst differs: minimum variance is significantly worse in the paper (−8.7%/yr, p 0.018) and in both Claude runs (claude science −7.1%/yr, p 0.036; Claude Code −7.5%/yr, p 0.028); in Grok's run score-aware MV was the worst; in ChatGPT's all four optimizers sit within 18.4–19.2% against 21.5% for 1/N with no paired test below p 0.33; Perplexity reports equal weight best on Sharpe (1.05 vs 0.84–0.95). In Claude Code the score-aware variants are 0.6–2.0 points *above* 1/N, not significantly (p 0.58–0.86) — a small divergence from the paper, where they sit below.
5. **Costs erode the edge at 1.1–1.2 CAGR points per 5 bps** in the four runs that report a cost table (ChatGPT 1.2, Perplexity 1.1, claude science 1.1, Claude Code 1.1; paper 1.1–1.2), on monthly turnover of 144–169% (paper 148%). For the ~25% builds, 25 bps leaves ~19.5% (still above SPY, not significantly) and 50 bps ~14% (below SPY); the 19–22% builds fall below SPY already at 25 bps. The paper still shows 23.0% at 50 bps.
6. **Size dominates.** Log market cap is the top SHAP feature in the paper and in the three replications that report SHAP with fundamentals present (Perplexity, claude science, Claude Code); ChatGPT's SHAP cannot show it. In Claude Code the next features are trend (SMA20/50 ratio, MACD), P/B and D/E; ROE ranks 11th and P/E 14th.

Two further paper claims replicate only in direction: walk-forward retraining is worse than the frozen model in claude science (22.5% vs 24.9%) but the paired penalty is small and not significant (−1.6%/yr, p 0.74; paper −8.8%/yr, p 0.015); and Ridge is far weaker than the tree models everywhere (13–17% CAGR), but the shortfall is not significant in claude science (p 0.17–0.25) and Claude Code's neural network matches the trees (30.5%, p 0.023), contradicting the paper's finding that non-tree learners fail.

## 3. Why the runs differ from each other

The CAGR ordering loosely follows the fundamentals block, which is the block the model leans on most — but only loosely:

| Fundamentals input | Run | CAGR | Sharpe | Mean IC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unknown | Kimi | 19.3% | 0.77 | — |
| None | ChatGPT | 21.5% | 0.93 | 0.016 |
| SEC XBRL, earlier-of filing/period-end+90d, back-filled pre-2009 | Perplexity | 22.4% | 1.05 | 0.020 |
| yfinance, sparse | Grok | 23.7% | 0.95 | 0.012 |
| SEC XBRL filing-date PIT, back-filled 2005–08 | claude science | 24.9% | 1.08 | 0.032 |
| SEC XBRL filing-date PIT 2009+, third-party quarterly 2004–08, no back-fill | Claude Code | 25.0% | 1.07 | 0.031 |

Grok, with sparse fundamentals, out-earns Perplexity's full XBRL build on CAGR while having the lowest IC of all, and the 3.5-point spread among the fundamentals-bearing runs is smaller than the 5.5-point spread that seed choice alone produces on one panel (§4). The ordering is suggestive, not established.

The two filing-date point-in-time builds (claude science and Claude Code) were built independently — different mirrors and parsers of the SEC filings, different pre-2009 sources, different price feeds — and both ran the authors' released code on the resulting panels. They agree on CAGR (24.9 vs 25.0), Sharpe (1.08 vs 1.07), alpha (8.4 vs 8.9) and IC (0.0315 vs 0.0311), while differing on drawdown (−12.4 vs −15.5) and Top−Bottom p (0.28 vs 0.15). Given the seed spread, this is consistency, not precision; it validates the data builds more than it pins the number. As a best estimate of what the published strategy does on public data: **CAGR ≈ 25%, Sharpe ≈ 1.07, SPY alpha ≈ 8.6%/yr, mean IC ≈ 0.031, Top−SPY p ≈ 0.06–0.08.**

Two runs need qualification:

- **ChatGPT omitted the five fundamental features, log market cap included** (64 features). Its 21.5% is an estimate for that specification, not a bound: its own variant without cross-asset proxies reaches 28.8% and its 2008+ pooled model 26.2%. Its learner ordering (Extra Trees 29.9% > Random Forest 28.1% > LightGBM 23.3% > XGBoost 21.5%) is not comparable with runs that had fundamentals; claude science's is Random Forest 27.2% > LightGBM 26.9% > XGBoost 24.9% > Extra Trees 24.1%, with the tree models statistically indistinguishable in paired tests. ChatGPT's package is otherwise the most carefully audited of the six (pinned upstream commit, every portfolio recomputed independently to 1e-16).
- **Kimi's "full-history variant" (28.4%, p 0.011)** is the only §1 row above 25% and the only one below p 0.05, but it is not the paper's specification and the message does not say what changed; the 23:21 message to Alan already flags it as "not the same OOS protocol". Treat it as a specification change until defined.

Smaller inconsistencies:

- **The short side is unstable.** Bottom20 CAGR is 13.4% (Claude Code), 14.0% (Perplexity), 16.3% (claude science) and 17.9% (ChatGPT) against the paper's 13.6% — above SPY in two of four. The long–short spread is 3.6–11.6 CAGR points versus the paper's 20.6, with most of the shortfall on the long side; Top−Bottom is not significant in any of the four runs that report it.
- **Regime-month counts.** ChatGPT and Perplexity reproduce the paper's 19/16/13/12 months per state; the Claude runs do not. This carries no information: the released `expanding_hmm_regimes.py` re-initialises hmmlearn at every refit, so numeric state IDs are not comparable across time (§4). Perplexity saw the symptom directly — its "crisis" state averaged a +16.5% annualized S&P return over 2021–25.
- **The HMM-feature sign flips between runs.** claude science's control explains part of it: against a model trained on 2008–2018 without dummies (29.2%), the dummies change the Top10 return by −1.3%/yr (aligned) or +0.9%/yr (raw), neither significant — so the gain of the "drop pre-2008" variants comes from discarding the back-filled 2005–07 rows. But ChatGPT's 2008+ pooled control gains 4.6 points with no fundamentals in the panel at all, so the post-2008 training window helps for reasons beyond fundamentals quality.
- **Cross-asset features.** The paper reports +3.0 CAGR points from them. claude science finds +0.8; Perplexity finds CAGR *higher* without them (23.1% vs 22.4%) though Sharpe lower; ChatGPT finds 28.8% without them versus 21.5% with (paired p 0.08) and attributes part of that to its proxies (including the negative April-2020 oil print). The claim does not replicate cleanly.

## 5. Why all six differ from the paper

1. **Data vendor, concentrated in the features that matter most.** Bloomberg's daily market cap, P/E, P/B, ROE and D/E are replaced by SEC-derived proxies (or omitted), and market cap, P/B and D/E are three of the four most important SHAP features in the runs that report them. claude science's control trained on 2008–2018 only — the years with genuine XBRL data — recovers 29.2% CAGR with p 0.007, and its 2019–20 validation window shows IC 0.089 (p 0.008). But restricting training to 2008+ also lifts ChatGPT's no-fundamentals model from 21.5% to 26.2%, so the training window matters at least as much as fundamentals quality; the two cannot be separated with these runs.
2. **Survivorship and size.** The universe is today's large caps. Within it a naive size rule matches the ranker (§4), and removing the 25 largest names barely changes the return (claude science 24.1%, Perplexity 24.8%) while improving IC significance — the signal is not carried by the mega-caps. Size is a benchmark the strategy has to beat, not yet shown to be the whole explanation.
3. **Sixty monthly observations and seed noise.** The paper's own robustness tables put the CAGR at 23.0% (walk-forward), 23.4% (per-regime models) and 31.3% (without cross-asset features); its 34.2% is the top of a wide range. Both seed experiments (§4) show the 5% threshold inside seed noise, and in claude science the shortfall is concentrated in two years — 2023 (19.5% vs the paper's 51.0%) and 2025 (10.7% vs SPY's 19.4%, mean IC −0.11). One bad year in five moves a 60-month p-value across 5%.
4. **Code issues surfaced by the replications.** The released alpha/beta formula mixes a sample covariance with a population variance, inflating beta by n/(n−1) (ChatGPT). The released feature lists enumerate 69 names while the paper reports 68 (every run that reports a count). The HMM script decodes the endpoint of a Viterbi path rather than the filtered state probability the paper describes, and re-initialises without aligning states (ChatGPT, claude science). Training labels near the 2018 boundary are not purged; purging them drops ChatGPT's CAGR from 21.5% to 18.3%. The 60 "monthly" observations are fixed 20-session windows that overlap or leave gaps (ChatGPT counts 8 overlaps, 37 gaps and 14 exact joins; its next-open calendar execution gives 25.9% rather than 21.5%). The 60-day-horizon "CAGR" compounds overlapping windows — a caveat the paper itself states (Table 17).

## 6. Bottom line

- **Replicates, where tested:** a tree-based ranker beats SPY in the point estimate (all six runs); regime information adds nothing significant as a feature or as a basis for per-regime models (four runs); no optimizer significantly beats equal weight (five runs); costs of 5 bps cost about 1.1–1.2 CAGR points (four runs); size is the dominant feature (three runs).
- **Does not replicate, or only in direction:** the magnitude (34% → 19–25% CAGR, Sharpe 1.28 → 0.77–1.08, alpha 13.6% → 1.5–8.9%); the 5%-level significance of every headline test, which turns out to depend on seed draw, learner and training window; the significant walk-forward penalty; the short side (Bottom20 above SPY in two of four runs); the cross-asset ablation (sign reversed on CAGR in two of three runs); the regime-conditional ordering (label switching); and the FF5+MOM alpha, at roughly half the paper's level.
- **Consensus reading:** a modest ranking signal (IC 0.01–0.03) that is positive in every run but not statistically distinguishable from zero in any of them; a best estimate from the two point-in-time builds of about 25% CAGR, Sharpe 1.07 gross and about 1.0 net of 5 bps (Claude Code 1.02, claude science 1.03, Perplexity 1.00, ChatGPT 0.88); Top−SPY p 0.06–0.32; and a naive size rule that matches it. That is a promising historical signal with no demonstrated live edge — the position the 23:24 message to Alan already took, now with the seed, size and label-switching claims independently confirmed.
