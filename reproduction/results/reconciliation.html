Reconciliation

Six AI replications of the regime-aware stock-ranking paper

Grok, ChatGPT, Kimi, Perplexity, "claude science" and Claude Code each re-ran Pascual Miralles & Alfeus (SSRN 7200998) on public data. Lined up against the paper and each other, they tell one consistent story.

Compiled from the Slack thread with Alan Coppola, 2026-09-25 (messages, screenshots, two markdown reports and the ChatGPT results package). Every figure was checked against its source by an independent review pass. Paper values are muted gray; each run keeps its own color throughout. Headline figures are gross of costs.

The headline numbers side by side

Out-of-sample 2021–2025, sixty monthly rebalances, equal-weight Top10 from a 7-seed XGBoost ensemble, gross of costs. SPY reproduces at 14.71% CAGR, 0.746 Sharpe and −20.47% drawdown in the five runs that report it (paper 14.72 / 0.746 / −20.47; Kimi does not report SPY), so the calendar, holding-period arithmetic and benchmark are right in those runs. The spread between runs comes from the feature panels — universe (205–206), feature count (64–69), rows (620k–663k) and above all the fundamentals — and the stock signal they produce.

Kimi's message is a five-row table with no protocol statement (assumed to follow the paper's Table 1 because its "Paper" column reproduces those values); its second row is a "full-history variant" that is not defined. A 988 MB results archive in the thread has no stated provenance. Grok reports "p ≈ 0.15" for beating SPY without naming the test and gives no stock, feature or row counts. Perplexity's report is marked interim (its alternative-model and walk-forward runs were still going) and its stock count is inferred. Claude Code loads 206 names but BK leaves the panel after April 2019, so its test universe is 205 like claude science's.

Multi-factor alphas, where run (annualized, HAC): paper FF5+MOM 17.68% (p 0.010); Perplexity 8.48% (p 0.007); ChatGPT 9.97% (p 0.038; CAPM 8.18%, p 0.093); claude science 7.66% (p 0.110; CAPM 10.27%, p 0.015). About half the paper's level; two of three nominally significant, none after the within-family corrections applied by claude science and ChatGPT.

What the runs agree on, where tested

  1. The Top10 portfolio beats SPY in the point estimate, by less than the paper says. Six of six runs: 19.3–25.0% CAGR against 14.7% for SPY — an excess of 4.6–10.3 CAGR points versus the paper's 19.5 (the paper's "17.24%" is an annualized mean-return excess). SPY alpha 1.5–8.9% versus 13.6%. Top−Random is positive wherever reported but never below p 0.067.
  2. The paper's 5%-level significance is not robust. With each run's closest match to the paper's specification, no Top−SPY, Top−Random, Top−Bottom or IC test is below 0.05 in any run: Top−SPY p 0.061–0.318 (paper 0.005), IC p 0.092–0.490 in the four runs that report it. Nominal 5% results appear once something changes — one of Perplexity's five seed sets and one of Claude Code's four; LightGBM and Random Forest in claude science, LightGBM/Random Forest/Extra Trees/neural network in Claude Code, Extra Trees only in ChatGPT; Kimi's variant; claude science's 2008–2018 training control (p 0.007); the FF5+MOM alphas of Perplexity and ChatGPT — and none survives the within-family corrections that claude science and ChatGPT applied.
  3. Regimes add nothing significant. Four runs tested it (ChatGPT, Perplexity, claude science, Claude Code; Grok could not because the released repo lacks the script, Kimi reported none). Paired annualized differences for "HMM regime as a feature" against each run's own baseline: −0.8 (Claude Code), +0.2 (ChatGPT), +1.0 (Perplexity), +2.2 to +4.4 (claude science, depending on the label-switched vs state-aligned regime series and on pre-2008 row handling); paper −2.9. None is significant (p 0.055–0.97). One model per regime: 12.2% (ChatGPT), 18.0% (Perplexity), 23.0–26.6% (claude science), 25.1% (Claude Code) against baselines of 21.5–25.0%, never significantly different, with a weaker IC wherever reported.
  4. No optimizer significantly beats 1/N inside the Top10. Five runs tested it (Kimi none). Which optimizer is worst differs: minimum variance is significantly worse in the paper (−8.7%/yr, p 0.018) and both Claude runs (claude science −7.1%/yr, p 0.036; Claude Code −7.5%/yr, p 0.028); in Grok's run score-aware MV was worst; in ChatGPT's all four sit within 18.4–19.2% against 21.5% with no paired test below p 0.33; Perplexity reports equal weight best on Sharpe (1.05 vs 0.84–0.95). In Claude Code the score-aware variants land 0.6–2.0 points above 1/N, not significantly.
  5. Costs erode the edge at 1.1–1.2 CAGR points per 5 bps in the four runs with a cost table (ChatGPT 1.2; Perplexity, claude science, Claude Code 1.1; paper 1.1–1.2), on 144–169% monthly turnover (paper 148%). For the ~25% builds, 25 bps leaves ~19.5% (above SPY, not significantly) and 50 bps ~14% (below SPY); the 19–22% builds drop below SPY already at 25 bps. The paper still shows 23.0% at 50 bps.
  6. Size dominates. Log market cap is the top SHAP feature in the paper and in the three replications that report SHAP with fundamentals present (Perplexity, claude science, Claude Code); ChatGPT's SHAP cannot show it. In Claude Code the next features are trend (SMA20/50, MACD), P/B and D/E; ROE ranks 11th and P/E 14th.

Two further paper claims replicate only in direction: walk-forward retraining is worse than the frozen model in claude science (22.5% vs 24.9%) but the penalty is small and not significant (−1.6%/yr, p 0.74; paper −8.8%/yr, p 0.015); and Ridge is far weaker than the trees everywhere (13–17%), but the shortfall is not significant in claude science (p 0.17–0.25) and Claude Code's neural network matches the trees (30.5%, p 0.023), contradicting the paper's finding that non-tree learners fail.

The two filing-date point-in-time builds (claude science and Claude Code) were built independently — different mirrors and parsers of the SEC filings, different pre-2009 sources, different price feeds — and both ran the authors' released code on the resulting panels. They agree on CAGR (24.9 vs 25.0), Sharpe (1.08 vs 1.07), alpha (8.4 vs 8.9) and IC (0.0315 vs 0.0311) while differing on drawdown (−12.4 vs −15.5) and Top−Bottom p (0.28 vs 0.15). Given the seed spread this is consistency, not precision; it validates the data builds more than it pins the number. Best estimate of what the published strategy does on public data: CAGR ≈ 25%, Sharpe ≈ 1.07, SPY alpha ≈ 8.6%/yr, mean IC ≈ 0.031, Top−SPY p ≈ 0.06–0.08.

Two runs need qualification

  • ChatGPT omitted the five fundamental features, log market cap included (64 features). Its 21.5% is an estimate for that specification, not a bound: its own variant without cross-asset proxies reaches 28.8% and its 2008+ pooled model 26.2%. Its learner ordering (Extra Trees 29.9% > Random Forest 28.1% > LightGBM 23.3% > XGBoost 21.5%) is not comparable with runs that had fundamentals; claude science's is Random Forest 27.2% > LightGBM 26.9% > XGBoost 24.9% > Extra Trees 24.1%, with the trees statistically indistinguishable in paired tests. ChatGPT's package is otherwise the most carefully audited of the six (pinned upstream commit, every portfolio recomputed independently to 1e-16).
  • Kimi's "full-history variant" (28.4%, p 0.011) is the only row above 25% and the only one below p 0.05, but it is not the paper's specification and the message does not say what changed (the 23:21 message to Alan already flags it as "not the same OOS protocol"). Treat it as a specification change until defined.

Smaller inconsistencies

  • The short side is unstable: Bottom20 CAGR is 13.4% (Claude Code), 14.0% (Perplexity), 16.3% (claude science) and 17.9% (ChatGPT) against the paper's 13.6% — above SPY in two of four. The long–short spread is 3.6–11.6 CAGR points versus the paper's 20.6, with most of the shortfall on the long side; Top−Bottom is not significant in any of the four runs that report it.
  • ChatGPT and Perplexity got the paper's exact regime-month counts (19/16/13/12); the Claude runs did not. This carries no information: the released HMM script re-initialises at every refit, so numeric state IDs are not comparable across time (next section). Perplexity saw the symptom directly — its "crisis" state averaged a +16.5% annualized S&P return over 2021–25.
  • The HMM-feature effect flips sign between runs. claude science's control explains part of it: against a model trained on 2008–2018 without dummies (29.2%), the dummies change the Top10 return by −1.3%/yr (aligned) or +0.9%/yr (raw), neither significant, so the gain of the "drop pre-2008" variants comes from discarding the back-filled 2005–07 rows. But ChatGPT's 2008+ control gains 4.6 points with no fundamentals in the panel, so the post-2008 window helps for reasons beyond fundamentals quality.
  • Cross-asset features: the paper reports +3.0 CAGR points from them; claude science finds +0.8, Perplexity finds CAGR higher without them (23.1% vs 22.4%) though Sharpe lower, and ChatGPT finds 28.8% without versus 21.5% with (paired p 0.08), attributing part of that to its proxies. The claim does not replicate cleanly.

Why all six differ from the paper

  1. Data vendor, concentrated in the features that matter most. Bloomberg's daily market cap, P/E, P/B, ROE and D/E are replaced by SEC-derived proxies or omitted, and market cap, P/B and D/E are three of the four most important SHAP features in the runs that report them. claude science's control trained on 2008–2018 only, the years with genuine XBRL data, recovers 29.2% CAGR with p 0.007, and its 2019–20 validation window shows IC 0.089 (p 0.008). But restricting training to 2008+ also lifts ChatGPT's no-fundamentals model from 21.5% to 26.2%, so the training window matters at least as much as fundamentals quality; these runs cannot separate the two.
  2. Survivorship and size. The universe is today's large caps. Within it a naive size rule matches the ranker, and removing the 25 largest names barely changes the return (claude science 24.1%, Perplexity 24.8%) while improving IC significance — the signal is not carried by the mega-caps. Size is a benchmark the strategy has to beat, not yet shown to be the whole explanation.
  3. Sixty monthly observations and seed noise. The paper's own robustness tables put the CAGR at 23.0% (walk-forward retraining), 23.4% (per-regime models) and 31.3% (without cross-asset features); the 34.2% headline is the top of a wide range. Both seed experiments show the 5% threshold inside seed noise — identical data, different seeds, CAGR anywhere from 23% to 29% — and in claude science the shortfall is concentrated in two years, 2023 (19.5% vs the paper's 51.0%) and 2025 (10.7% vs SPY's 19.4%, mean IC −0.11). One bad year in five moves a 60-month p-value across 5%.
  4. Code issues surfaced by the replications. The released alpha/beta formula mixes a sample covariance with a population variance, inflating beta by n/(n−1) (ChatGPT). The feature lists enumerate 69 names while the paper reports 68 (every run that reports a count). The HMM script decodes the endpoint of a Viterbi path rather than the filtered state probability the paper describes, and re-initialises without aligning states (ChatGPT, claude science). Training labels near the 2018 boundary are not purged; purging them drops ChatGPT's CAGR from 21.5% to 18.3%. The sixty "monthly" observations are fixed 20-session windows that overlap or leave gaps (ChatGPT counts 8 overlaps, 37 gaps, 14 exact joins; its next-open calendar execution gives 25.9% rather than 21.5%). The 60-day-horizon "CAGR" compounds overlapping windows — a caveat the paper itself states in Table 17.

Bottom line

Replicates, where tested: a tree-based ranker beats SPY in the point estimate (all six runs); regime information adds nothing significant as a feature or as a basis for per-regime models (four runs); no optimizer significantly beats equal weight (five runs); 5 bps of costs remove about 1.1–1.2 CAGR points (four runs); size is the dominant feature (three runs).

Does not replicate, or only in direction: the magnitude (34% → 19–25% CAGR, Sharpe 1.28 → 0.77–1.08, alpha 13.6% → 1.5–8.9%); the 5%-level significance of every headline test, which turns out to depend on seed draw, learner and training window; the significant walk-forward penalty; the short side (Bottom20 above SPY in two of four runs); the cross-asset ablation (sign reversed on CAGR in two of three runs); the regime-conditional ordering (label switching); and the FF5+MOM alpha, at roughly half the paper's level.

Consensus reading: a modest ranking signal (IC 0.01–0.03) that is positive in every run but not statistically distinguishable from zero in any of them; a best estimate from the two point-in-time builds of about 25% CAGR, Sharpe 1.07 gross and about 1.0 net of 5 bps (Claude Code 1.02, claude science 1.03, Perplexity 1.00, ChatGPT 0.88); Top−SPY p 0.06–0.32; and a naive size rule that matches it. A promising historical signal with no demonstrated live edge — the position the 23:24 message to Alan already took, now with the seed, size and label-switching claims independently confirmed.
